## What produces movement

Muscles produce force and transmit it through tendons to move or stabilize joints. The resistance you experience depends on the load, body position and its distance from the joint. A squat and a leg press can challenge overlapping muscles while remaining different skills.

## What changes with practice

Early improvements can include learning the movement and coordinating effort. Muscle growth may also contribute over time. More repetitions at a given load demonstrate improved performance under those conditions; they do not measure how much muscle you gained.

## Make the comparison useful

Keep the exercise, range of motion and rest reasonably similar when comparing sessions. Record changes in setup rather than hiding them. Using a lighter load through a newly comfortable range can be a productive practice decision even when the number in the log decreases.

## Evidence & reading

1. <span id="ref-R20"></span>[ACSM resistance training position stand (2026)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/)
Overview of human intervention reviews. Population:  Healthy adults. Endpoint: Strength, muscle size and physical performance.Limit: Group evidence does not establish one optimal program for every beginner.
2. <span id="ref-R23"></span>[Currier et al. Resistance training prescriptions (2023)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/)
Systematic review and network meta-analysis of human trials. Population:  Healthy adults. Endpoint: Strength and hypertrophy.Limit: Comparisons across trials are not a personalized prescription.
3. <span id="ref-R25"></span>[Mitchell et al. Acute MPS and hypertrophy (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24586775/)
Human intervention / tracer study. Population:  Young men undertaking resistance training. Endpoint: Acute synthesis and later hypertrophy.Limit: One acute measurement did not predict individual growth in this study; other settings may differ.
