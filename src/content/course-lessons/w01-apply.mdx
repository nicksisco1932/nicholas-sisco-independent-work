Allow roughly 8–15 minutes with the activity. Suggested prerequisite: [Adaptation starts with a question](w01-understand.html).

## Keep the record small

For three ordinary days, note one movement opportunity, approximate sleep duration and a brief description of meals. Add a constraint such as a long commute or lack of cooking equipment. No body weight, calorie total or wearable is required. Leave uncertain values as estimates.

## Use the same language

Describe movement by activity and duration rather than a watch’s calorie estimate. For sleep, distinguish time in bed from estimated time asleep. For food, write what was available and satisfying instead of labeling the day good or bad. Consistent definitions make comparisons more meaningful.

## Choose a workable next step

Do not deliberately maintain an uncomfortable or unsafe routine just to finish a baseline. You may improve something immediately and note the change. The purpose is to make your next decision easier, not to create perfect experimental control. If logging feels burdensome, use a fictional example instead.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>Day 1: a 12-minute walk, about 6.5 hours asleep, breakfast skipped because the bus arrived early. A useful response is to prepare a portable breakfast option or change the morning schedule—not conclude that skipping one meal damaged metabolism.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Use the baseline sheet for three days, or reconstruct three typical days from memory.
- Circle one repeated constraint and write one small change that addresses it.

Scenario alternative: Complete the sheet for a fictional commuter with a limited food budget.
