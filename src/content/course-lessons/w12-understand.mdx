## Organize the next question

A training cycle is a period over which you organize goals, exposures and review. It can be simple. For a beginner, repeatable sessions and manageable progression often matter more than an elaborate sequence of named phases.

## Give each period a purpose

You might first stabilize attendance and technique, then gradually progress familiar movements. A reduced-demand period can be included if useful, followed by a review. These are planning choices, not a guaranteed sequence of biological states.

## Check the arithmetic

If you call a plan twelve weeks, its parts must total twelve. Leave enough room for disruptions rather than presenting a schedule as an obligation. Review points can occur without maximal tests or compulsory body measurements.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>An example divides twelve weeks into four weeks of consistent practice, four of gradual progression, one optional reduced-demand week and three of consolidation and review: 4 + 4 + 1 + 3 = 12.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Assign a purpose to each period in the example.
- Describe when you would change the schedule rather than follow it rigidly.

Scenario alternative: Use a six-week planning horizon if that is easier; check that its parts sum to six.
