## Read the complete claim

Identify the ingredient, amount, population, comparison and outcome. A statement about a cell pathway does not establish that an oral product improves performance in humans. Even a human study may measure only a short-term biomarker rather than a useful training outcome.

## Separate ingredient and product evidence

Evidence for an ingredient under specified conditions does not validate every mixture containing it. A product may provide a different amount, include other ingredients or use an untested formulation. Manufacturing quality and effectiveness are separate questions.

## Ask whether the result matters here

Some ingredients have evidence for particular tasks; that does not make them necessary for a beginner. Creatine research includes repeated high-intensity performance and training contexts. Caffeine effects depend on the task and tolerance, and sleep or side effects may alter the practical decision. Neither observation establishes a universal course requirement.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>A label says an ingredient increases a cellular signal. The advertised conclusion is larger muscles in four weeks. The missing evidence is a relevant human trial measuring muscle size over time.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Identify whether its evidence concerns mechanism, performance, size or product quality.
