Allow roughly 8–15 minutes with the activity. Suggested prerequisite: [A plan has to survive its environment](w10-understand.html).

## Set the primary plan

Choose a realistic schedule using the starter program or a suitable established routine. Identify where food preparation and rest fit. Be specific enough that you can tell whether the plan occurred, but avoid turning every hour into a compliance test.

## Define the fallback

Choose a smaller version for the likely interruption. For example, one set of the familiar movements may fit a short slot. A ready-to-eat meal can replace cooking when safely available. Rest may be the correct alternative when illness or symptoms make exercise inappropriate.

## Plan the return

A fallback is not a debt. Return to the next appropriate session without doubling the work. Review whether the primary plan needs redesign if the fallback is repeatedly necessary. The goal is a workable routine, not preserving an unrealistic calendar.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>A two-session week includes a twenty-minute option for a late workday and a home option for lost transport. If both sessions are missed, the next week begins at a manageable level instead of four catch-up sessions.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Write the primary plan, the likely interruption and the fallback.
- Choose a review date and a condition that would make you redesign the primary plan.
