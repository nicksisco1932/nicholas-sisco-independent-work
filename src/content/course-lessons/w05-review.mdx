Allow roughly 10–15 minutes with the activity. Suggested prerequisite: [Make an ordinary-day and difficult-day plan](w05-apply.html).

## Separate what happened from why

Write observations first: three missed meals, two shorter nights, lower repetitions. Then list plausible explanations. Avoid turning their sequence into certainty. Training, work, illness and food access can interact.

## Evaluate the adjustment

Ask whether the change was workable and whether everyday function or performance returned toward usual. A helpful response supports continuing the plan provisionally. It still does not prove which biological process changed.

## Know the limit of the worksheet

Persistent, unexplained fatigue or sleep problems call for professional assessment. Urgent symptoms such as chest pain, fainting or severe unusual breathlessness require urgent care. A course checklist cannot rule out illness.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>Casey’s performance improves after reducing volume and restoring regular meals. Casey can say the combined plan was useful, but cannot conclude that cortisol was the original problem.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Write an observation, a hypothesis and an action as three separate sentences.
- Decide what would make you continue the adjustment versus seek help.

Scenario alternative: Use Casey’s example and name two explanations that remain possible.
