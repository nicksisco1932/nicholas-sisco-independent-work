Allow roughly 8–15 minutes with the activity. Suggested prerequisite: [A plausible mechanism is the beginning](w08-understand.html).

## Extract before evaluating

Find the actual study or an authoritative evidence summary. Record the study type, participants, duration, dose as studied, comparison and endpoint. If the advertisement provides no accessible source, label the evidence unavailable rather than filling the gap with assumptions.

## Check relevance and limitations

Ask whether participants resemble the intended user and whether the outcome matches the promise. Note sample size, uncertainty, adverse effects and funding when reported. A statistically detectable effect may still be small or irrelevant to your task.

## Include the opportunity cost

Calculate cost per usable serving and per month using the label’s stated use, solely as an arithmetic exercise. Compare it with an unmet practical need such as an affordable meal or access to suitable equipment. Choosing not to purchase is a complete decision.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>A fictional $30 container provides thirty labeled servings: $1 each, or $30 for thirty uses. An ingredient study alone does not establish that this $30 mixture works.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Create a claim sheet with population, endpoint, duration, comparison and limitations.
- Calculate the fictional cost and write a purchase-independent conclusion.
