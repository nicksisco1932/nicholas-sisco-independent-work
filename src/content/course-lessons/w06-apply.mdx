Allow roughly 8–15 minutes with the activity. Suggested prerequisite: [Timing depends on the job](w06-understand.html).

## Start from familiar foods

Use the meal-builder worksheet to identify a protein-containing food, carbohydrate source, produce and other foods that make the meal satisfying and practical. These are planning prompts, not a guarantee of nutrient adequacy. Include dietary restrictions, storage and budget.

## Place food around the actual schedule

Write when you can realistically eat. If the last meal is far from training and hunger makes the session difficult, consider a familiar snack beforehand. Plan a normal meal afterward when practical. Do not create anxiety about missing a narrow muscle-building window.

## Make the backup usable

A no-cook option must be ready to eat or safely prepared in advance. Dry rice and uncooked beans are ingredients, not immediate meals. Shelf-stable options, leftovers or convenience foods can be useful depending on access and food safety.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>An evening plan uses a usual lunch, a yogurt or suitable alternative before training if hungry, and dinner afterward. The delayed-work backup uses an available ready-to-eat meal and a shorter session.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Fill two meal-builder schedules using foods you can obtain and tolerate.
- Check whether the backup needs cooking, refrigeration or equipment you will actually have.

Scenario alternative: Design schedules for the evening-training example using a limited grocery budget.
