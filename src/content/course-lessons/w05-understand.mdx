## Several processes share the word

Recovery can mean restored ability to perform, replenished fuel, resolution of soreness or a return to usual sleep and mood. These processes do not follow a single clock. Feeling ready does not prove every tissue has recovered, and soreness does not quantify growth.

## Sleep has a role without being a score

Adult sleep guidance generally recommends at least seven hours, with individual variation. Duration, regularity, opportunity and symptoms all matter. A watch estimate can prompt a question; it cannot identify a hormone disorder or precisely measure readiness for lifting.

## Look for a pattern

Compare several observations: usual tasks, appetite, sleep opportunity, fatigue and session performance. Persistent deterioration deserves attention. One poor night is a reason to consider the day’s demands, not proof that adaptation has stopped.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>Rae feels sore after trying a new exercise but sleeps normally and daily movement is comfortable. Lee reports several weeks of worsening fatigue and declining performance. These patterns call for different responses.</p></aside>
