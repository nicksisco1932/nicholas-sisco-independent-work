Allow roughly 10–15 minutes with the activity. Suggested prerequisite: [Build a three-day baseline](w01-apply.html).

## Make the action concrete

A useful experiment specifies what, when and how much. 'Walk for ten minutes after lunch on Tuesday and Thursday' is easier to evaluate than 'be more disciplined.' Choose an action you can change if it does not fit. Early success means learning what is workable.

## Separate behavior from outcome

You can schedule a session; you cannot guarantee a particular strength gain by Friday. Record whether the plan happened and what interfered. A missed attempt reveals a design problem or competing demand, not a fixed character flaw.

## Review rather than escalate automatically

At the end of the week, keep, simplify or replace the experiment. If it worked, repeat it before making it harder. General activity and sleep recommendations give context; they do not require reaching every target immediately.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>Plan: two ten-minute walks after lunch. Fallback: five minutes before leaving work. Review: Sunday. If meetings repeatedly eliminate lunch, move the cue rather than adding guilt or doubling the next walk.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Write a primary plan, a smaller fallback and a review date.
- Explain which observation would lead you to keep the plan and which would lead you to change it.
