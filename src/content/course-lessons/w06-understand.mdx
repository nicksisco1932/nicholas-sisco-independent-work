## Start with the whole day

Food supplies energy, essential amino acids and other nutrients. Consistent access to enough food is the foundation of planning. A perfectly timed snack cannot compensate for a repeatedly inadequate day. Protein timing is one consideration within total intake, food quality, preferences and training.

## Ask about duration and turnaround

A short recreational strength session and a prolonged endurance event place different demands on fuel planning. Rapid carbohydrate replenishment is more relevant when demanding sessions are close together than when a beginner trains on separate days.

## Keep tolerance in the plan

A familiar meal before training may suit one person; another may prefer a smaller snack. Very large meals close to movement can be uncomfortable for some people. There is no universal rule that all meals near exercise must exclude fat or fiber.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>Priya lifts for forty minutes after lunch and trains again two days later. An athlete completing two prolonged sessions the same day has a different recovery interval and should not define Priya’s meal rules.</p></aside>
