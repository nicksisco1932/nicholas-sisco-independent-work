## Follow the levels

Loading creates forces that muscle cells sense. Signaling networks can then influence processes such as protein synthesis. Repeated changes in synthesis and breakdown contribute to tissue remodeling. A larger muscle, a better practiced lift and improved whole-body performance are related but different outcomes.

## Keep materials in the explanation

Amino acids are needed to make proteins. Leucine participates in nutrient sensing, but leucine alone is not all the material required to construct new muscle protein. A pathway can be permissive or contributory without being sufficient to produce the final outcome.

## Look at the endpoint

A phosphorylation measurement, an acute tracer estimate of synthesis and a months-long strength test answer different questions. You cannot substitute the first for the last. Beginner strength changes can also include learning and coordination; improved performance does not by itself quantify muscle growth.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>Two people show a molecular response after training. That does not tell you which will gain more muscle over twelve weeks. The relevant follow-up includes repeated training, food intake, measurement methods and the actual tissue or performance endpoint.</p></aside>
