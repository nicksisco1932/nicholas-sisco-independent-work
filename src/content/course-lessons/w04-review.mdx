## Read the whole entry

A useful review asks whether technique, range, effort and rest stayed similar. Extra repetitions after a longer rest may still be worthwhile, but they do not establish the same comparison. Fatigue from earlier exercises can also change a later set.

## Decide rather than judge

Choose among repeat, small progression or regression. Repeat when the evidence is mixed. Progress when the agreed criteria are met. Reduce the demand when control or recovery is deteriorating. None of these decisions establishes a person’s discipline or long-term potential.

## Update the question

If a movement remains awkward, the next question may be about instruction or equipment rather than effort. A coach can help assess technique; a clinician is appropriate for persistent symptoms. The log should support a decision rather than supply a diagnosis.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>Jo reaches twelve repetitions once but shortens the range on the last three. Jo repeats the prior load with the original comfortable range instead of adding weight.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Review two real or fictional sessions and choose repeat, progress or regress.
- Write one sentence explaining what the log cannot establish about hypertrophy.

Scenario alternative: Use Jo’s case and describe what you would look for at the next session.

## Evidence & reading

1. <span id="ref-R20"></span>[ACSM resistance training position stand (2026)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/)
Overview of human intervention reviews. Population:  Healthy adults. Endpoint: Strength, muscle size and physical performance.Limit: Group evidence does not establish one optimal program for every beginner.
2. <span id="ref-R23"></span>[Currier et al. Resistance training prescriptions (2023)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37414459/)
Systematic review and network meta-analysis of human trials. Population:  Healthy adults. Endpoint: Strength and hypertrophy.Limit: Comparisons across trials are not a personalized prescription.
3. <span id="ref-R25"></span>[Mitchell et al. Acute MPS and hypertrophy (2014)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24586775/)
Human intervention / tracer study. Population:  Young men undertaking resistance training. Endpoint: Acute synthesis and later hypertrophy.Limit: One acute measurement did not predict individual growth in this study; other settings may differ.
