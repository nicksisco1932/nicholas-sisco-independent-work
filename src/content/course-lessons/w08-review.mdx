Allow roughly 10–15 minutes with the activity. Suggested prerequisite: [Audit the claim on paper](w08-apply.html).

## Use bounded language

A good conclusion names the tested context. It might say that an ingredient has evidence for a particular performance task in studied adults, while this formulation and this individual response remain untested. Avoid replacing uncertainty with either enthusiasm or blanket dismissal.

## Do not turn a trial into a diagnosis

Feeling different after starting a product does not isolate its cause. Training, expectations, sleep and ordinary variation may change at the same time. Self-experimentation also cannot rule out interactions or contamination.

## Leave clinical questions to appropriate care

Disease treatment, medication replacement, pediatric dosing and deficiency management are outside this course. Ask a qualified clinician or pharmacist about individual risks or interactions rather than assuming a product is safe because it is sold without prescription.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>A small acute study reports a signaling change but no strength or size measurement. A defensible conclusion is that the study supports that measured response under its conditions; the advertised long-term result was not tested.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Write a supported conclusion and a separate unsupported conclusion for the example.
- List the study you would need to test the advertised long-term claim.

Scenario alternative: Keep the exercise entirely on paper and use a fictional ingredient.
