## Energy is an accounting concept

Food provides energy and material. Over time, a mismatch between absorbed energy and expenditure contributes to changes in stored body energy. Both intake and expenditure vary. Short-term body weight also reflects water and digestive contents, so it is not a direct daily readout of fat change.

## Food also supplies materials

Protein supplies amino acids; fats contribute essential fatty acids; carbohydrate can support demanding activity. Foods also supply fiber and micronutrients. Adequate calories alone do not establish adequate nutrition, and a nutrient-dense menu can still provide too little energy for a person’s needs.

## Use numbers with a population attached

Protein guidance for healthy exercising adults is not a universal minimum for everyone. For example, the ISSN position stand discusses 1.4–2.0 g per kg of body weight per day for most exercising people. This uses total body weight, not measured lean mass. Clinical conditions may require different guidance.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>For a hypothetical healthy 70 kg exerciser, 1.4–2.0 g/kg/day corresponds to 98–140 g/day. This demonstrates units and arithmetic; it is not your assigned intake. Four meals of 25 g supply 100 g, but neither that meal count nor one number suits everyone.</p></aside>
