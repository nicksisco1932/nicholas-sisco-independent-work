## Name the actual constraint

Time, money, transport, equipment, disability, caregiving and food availability shape what can be done. A plan that ignores those conditions is incomplete. Missing it does not prove low motivation or a failure of identity.

## Keep the purpose, change the route

Identify what a planned action is meant to accomplish. A training session might provide practice with several movement patterns. A shorter version may preserve some of that purpose, although it is not automatically an equivalent dose. A convenient meal may solve an access problem even when it differs from the idealized menu.

## Treat the plan as a design choice

Use an if–then statement to specify your alternative: if the gym is unavailable, then I will use the familiar home version. This is a practical organizing tool. It is not evidence that a sentence alone will change behavior or overcome structural barriers.

- Write an alternative that preserves part of the original purpose.
