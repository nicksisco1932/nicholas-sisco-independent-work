## A useful signal with many causes

Fatigue can arise during a session, accumulate across demands or accompany illness. A difficult week cannot establish overtraining syndrome. That diagnosis involves prolonged performance impairment and assessment of other possible causes; there is no single everyday score that confirms it.

## Look beyond the workout

Training is one demand among work, caregiving, sleep disruption and food access. Review the pattern before deciding that your metabolism is damaged or that your nervous system needs a reset. Those explanations claim more than a training log can show.

## Act at the right scale

For an ordinary difficult day, reduce or postpone the demand. Persistent unexplained deterioration, recurring pain or symptoms affecting daily life deserve assessment. Chest pain, fainting or severe unusual breathlessness warrant urgent attention rather than another self-test.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>One lower-repetition session after a stressful day is different from several weeks of declining performance and unusual exhaustion despite easier training.</p></aside>
