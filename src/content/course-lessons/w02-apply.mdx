## Start with the serving

A nutrition label describes a stated quantity. Comparing grams of protein on two packages without comparing serving sizes can reverse the result. Use the same food weight, an actual portion, or the total usable package—whichever answers your question.

## Use more than one column

Cost per portion, protein per portion, fiber, preparation time, storage and enjoyment answer different questions. Keep them separate. Adding grams per dollar to a subjective satiety score does not create a meaningful health score. An unfamiliar ingredient name is not evidence that a food is harmful.

## Build a meal, not a ranking

Choose a protein-containing food, a source of carbohydrate as appropriate, produce, some fat and flavor. Foods can fill more than one role: beans provide protein, carbohydrate and fiber. These categories are a planning aid, not a rule that every plate must contain five separate ingredients.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>Illustrative labels: A provides 12 g protein per 150 g portion; B provides 8 g per 100 g. Both provide 8 g per 100 g. Price, portion preference and other nutrients—not the larger printed protein number—can decide the choice.</p></aside>

## Try it

- Compare two labels after putting them on the same serving basis.
- Build one meal with a substitution that fits a different budget, culture or dietary preference.

## Evidence & reading

1. <span id="ref-R19"></span>[FDA, How to Understand and Use the Nutrition Facts Label](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/how-understand-and-use-nutrition-facts-label)
Government educational guidance. Population:  US food-label users. Endpoint: Serving-size and nutrient comparisons.Limit: Label-based comparisons need consistent units; ingredient familiarity is not a validated health score.
2. <span id="ref-R24"></span>[ISSN position stand: protein and exercise (2017)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/)
Position stand / synthesis. Population:  Healthy exercising people. Endpoint: Protein intake and adaptation.Limit: Athletic guidance differs from minimum adequacy and clinical needs.
3. <span id="ref-R01"></span>[Morton et al. (2018), protein supplementation and resistance training](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608)
Human intervention synthesis. Population:  Healthy adults undertaking resistance training. Endpoint: Fat-free mass and strength.Limit: Group-level dose-response estimate; not a universal minimum, not a lean-mass denominator.
