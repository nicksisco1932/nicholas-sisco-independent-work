## The scientific question

How does leucine availability become a cellular response, and where does the evidence stop short of hypertrophy?

### What changed

Foundational synthesis for the atlas launch; this article is not reporting a newly published study.

<aside class="callout"><h2>The foundation</h2><p>Muscle cells need raw materials, energy, and instructions to make proteins. Leucine participates as both a building block and a signal. Neither role replaces the need for other amino acids, and new protein can support repair rather than growth.</p></aside>

<h2 id="what-leucine-is">What leucine is</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

Leucine is an essential amino acid: a chemical building block that the human body cannot make in adequate amounts. Dietary proteins deliver leucine together with other amino acids.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Digestion and transport determine availability to tissues. Inside cells, leucine can enter protein synthesis or participate in metabolism and sensing.

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — What leucine is</summary><p>An amino acid's concentration is a state variable, not a flux. Plasma concentration, intracellular free pools, transport, oxidation, and incorporation need not change in parallel.</p></details>

<p class="kicker">02</p>

<h2 id="why-sensing-exists">Why amino-acid sensing exists</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

Making a protein requires a complete set of ingredients. Cells benefit from coordinating construction with what is available.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

The nutrient arm of mTORC1 regulation connects amino-acid availability to the location of a regulatory complex. [Sancak Y 2008](#ref-sancak2008)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Why amino-acid sensing exists</summary><p>A sensing pathway conveys information; a substrate pool supplies matter. A sensor responding to one amino acid cannot establish that all substrates and energy are sufficient.</p></details>

<p class="kicker">03</p>

<h2 id="sestrin2">Sestrin2</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

A molecule can carry information by binding to a protein. Sestrin2 is one example of a leucine-responsive binding protein. [Wolfson RL 2016](#ref-wolfson2016)

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Leucine occupancy changes Sestrin2's interaction with GATOR2, linking nutrient availability to regulation upstream of the Rags. [Wolfson RL 2016](#ref-wolfson2016)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Sestrin2</summary><p>For an ideal single-site equilibrium, fractional occupancy is θ = [free ligand] / (Kd + [free ligand]). This illustrative binding model is not a fitted description of exercising muscle: competing interactions, compartmental concentrations, kinetics, and sensor abundance can alter the cellular response. A plasma leucine concentration cannot be substituted for the free concentration at a sensor. Nor does occupancy establish that Sestrin2 is the sole relevant human muscle sensor. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-wolfson2016">Wolfson RL 2016</a></span></p></details>

<p class="kicker">04</p>

<h2 id="rag-gtpases">Rag GTPases</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

Proteins often need to meet before they can influence one another. The Rags help organize such encounters.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Rag heterodimers interact with RAPTOR to recruit mTORC1 in response to nutrient information. [Sancak Y 2008](#ref-sancak2008)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Rag GTPases</summary><p>RagA/B–GTP paired with RagC/D–GDP favors recruitment. The states of both partners matter; calling all GTPases simply 'on when GTP-bound' obscures this asymmetry. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-docking2019">Rogala KB 2019</a></span></p></details>

<p class="kicker">05</p>

<h2 id="lysosomal-localization">Lysosomal localization of mTORC1</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

A lysosome is a membrane-bound recycling compartment that also serves as a signaling location.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Lysosome-associated Rag machinery positions mTORC1 for productive regulatory encounters. The membrane surface is part of the mechanism. [Rogala KB 2019](#ref-docking2019)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Lysosomal localization of mTORC1</summary><p>Recruitment alters effective local concentration and encounter geometry. A membrane constrains movement and orientation, so bulk concentration does not completely specify productive molecular encounters. Colocalization in an image supports proximity at the assay's resolution, not necessarily binding or enzyme activity. Distinguishing recruitment from activation requires orthogonal measurements of interaction and catalysis. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-docking2019">Rogala KB 2019</a></span></p></details>

<p class="kicker">06</p>

<h2 id="rheb">Rheb and permissive activation</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

Being in the right location is only part of the story. An enzyme can still need a separate activating interaction.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Rheb in its GTP-bound state promotes mTORC1 catalysis. TSC provides restraint on this input and connects it to other regulatory conditions. [Yang H 2017](#ref-yang2017); [Inoki K 2002](#ref-inoki2002)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Rheb and permissive activation</summary><p>Rheb acts through allosteric structural change. 'Permissive' describes the cellular context; biochemically Rheb is a direct activator, not merely a passive docking factor. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-yang2017">Yang H 2017</a></span></p></details>

<p class="kicker">07</p>

<h2 id="s6k1-4ebp1">S6K1 / 4E-BP1</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

mTORC1 influences other proteins that help control the protein-making machinery.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

S6K1 is a downstream kinase. 4E-BP1 regulates eIF4E availability and initiation-factor assembly; these are distinct branches, not consecutive steps. [Drummond MJ 2009](#ref-drummond2009); [Thoreen CC 2012](#ref-thoreen2012)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — S6K1 / 4E-BP1</summary><p>Phosphorylation is site-specific and reversible. Protein abundance, subcellular location, substrate access, and phosphatases influence the meaning of a measured phosphosite.</p></details>

<p class="kicker">08</p>

<h2 id="translation-initiation">Translation initiation</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

The cell assembles machinery to start reading a messenger RNA before it can build a protein.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Changes in eIF4E binding help regulate cap-dependent initiation. Regulation differs across messages. [Thoreen CC 2012](#ref-thoreen2012)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Translation initiation</summary><p>Initiation does not bypass elongation constraints. Charging a transfer RNA couples amino-acid attachment to ATP conversion to AMP and pyrophosphate; elongation also uses GTP-dependent steps. Thus an initiation signal cannot remove energetic or substrate constraints. Ribosome occupancy alone can also be ambiguous: more ribosomes on an RNA might reflect faster initiation or slower movement along that RNA.</p></details>

<p class="kicker">09</p>

<h2 id="signaling-is-not-growth">Why signaling is not the same as hypertrophy</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

A message to build is different from a completed structure, and a completed protein may replace an old one.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Net accretion depends on synthesis and breakdown integrated over time. A short post-exercise response may include remodeling rather than lasting enlargement. [Damas F 2016](#ref-damas2016)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Why signaling is not the same as hypertrophy</summary><p>One study found that an initial acute myofibrillar synthesis response did not correlate with subsequent hypertrophy. This tests a proposed predictor, not whether synthesis is biologically required. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-mitchell2014">Mitchell CJ 2014</a></span></p></details>

<p class="kicker">10</p>

<h2 id="all-essential-amino-acids">Why total essential amino acids still matter</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

A protein sequence needs many amino acids. Adding more of one ingredient does not manufacture the missing ones.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Leucine can influence a feeding response, but the full amino-acid mixture and time window matter for sustained synthesis. [Churchward-Venne TA 2012](#ref-churchward2012)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Why total essential amino acids still matter</summary><p>Existing intracellular pools and protein recycling can temporarily supply amino acids. Their presence explains why an acute response does not establish long-term nutritional sufficiency.</p></details>

<p class="kicker">11</p>

<h2 id="energetic-state">Interaction with energetic state</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

Protein construction consumes energy. Cells coordinate it with other demands rather than following nutrient signals in isolation.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

AMPK supplies an energy-sensitive regulatory input and can restrain mTORC1 through RAPTOR phosphorylation. [Gwinn DM 2008](#ref-gwinn2008)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Interaction with energetic state</summary><p>This inhibitory connection is experimentally supported in cellular systems. It does not make AMPK an explanation by itself for concurrent-training outcomes or a binary antagonist of all growth. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-gwinn2008">Gwinn DM 2008</a></span></p></details>

<p class="kicker">12</p>

<h2 id="resistance-training">Interaction with resistance training</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

Training loads the tissue that must adapt. Feeding supplies resources and regulatory inputs in the context of that exposure.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Mechanical input has its own routes to signaling; a loading diagram should not force every response through the leucine sensor. [Hornberger TA 2006](#ref-hornberger2006)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Interaction with resistance training</summary><p>Ex vivo mechanical experiments can isolate candidate mechanisms. Human exercise combines recruitment, force transmission, metabolism, and systemic responses, making direct translation less simple. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-hornberger2006">Hornberger TA 2006</a></span></p></details>

<p class="kicker">13</p>

<h2 id="human-evidence">What is strongly supported in humans</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

Researchers can measure newly synthesized muscle proteins after feeding or exercise using labeled tracers and biopsies. [Churchward-Venne TA 2012](#ref-churchward2012)

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Pharmacological inhibition with rapamycin blocked an early post-exercise synthesis increase, supporting a role for rapamycin-sensitive regulation in that protocol. [Drummond MJ 2009](#ref-drummond2009)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — What is strongly supported in humans</summary><p>These are bounded findings: the intervention, participants, protein pool, and recovery interval define the claim. They do not identify every intervening sensor or prove a chronic supplement effect. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-drummond2009">Drummond MJ 2009</a></span></p></details>

<p class="kicker">14</p>

<h2 id="open-questions">What remains mechanistically uncertain</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

We know many parts of the system, but not exactly how much each part explains a person's adaptation.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

Sensor contributions, spatial organization, translational capacity, and turnover must be connected across timescales. Human ribosomal associations and cell-culture necessity tests answer different questions. [Stec MJ 2016](#ref-stec2016)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — What remains mechanistically uncertain</summary><p>A stronger translational chain would connect a specific perturbation to molecular state, measured synthesis and breakdown, repeated exposure, tissue change, and function in the same relevant population. The map is a guide to those tests, not a substitute for them.</p></details>

## Advanced mechanism in context

The core distinction is recruitment versus catalytic activation versus biosynthetic flux. Rag-dependent positioning, Rheb-dependent allostery, and downstream translation regulation connect these levels without making them equivalent. [Sancak Y 2008](#ref-sancak2008); [Yang H 2017](#ref-yang2017); [Thoreen CC 2012](#ref-thoreen2012)

## Why it matters

It prevents a plausible molecular explanation from being mistaken for proof that an intervention produces lasting human muscle growth.

<h2 id="appraisal">Evidence & limits</h2>

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Human intervention</span><span>Tracer study</span></div><p class="small">Model: Human · Directness: Direct</p><p>Feeding and exercise studies measure acute synthesis responses, not the full molecular chain. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-churchward2012">Churchward-Venne TA 2012</a>; <a href="#ref-drummond2009">Drummond MJ 2009</a></span></p></div>

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Cell culture</span><span>Biochemical reconstitution</span></div><p class="small">Model: Mixed · Directness: Direct</p><p>Binding, docking, and catalytic mechanisms are tested in experimental model systems. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-wolfson2016">Wolfson RL 2016</a>; <a href="#ref-docking2019">Rogala KB 2019</a>; <a href="#ref-yang2017">Yang H 2017</a></span></p></div>

- **What the studies demonstrated**: The cited studies establish defined cellular sensing mechanisms and acute human feeding or exercise responses. They measure different links in the proposed chain.
- **What they did not demonstrate**: No single experiment here traces dietary leucine through every molecular node to long-term hypertrophy in humans.
- **Study type**: Narrative synthesis of selected primary experiments; not a systematic review.
- **Population / model**: Human feeding and training cohorts, cultured cells, isolated animal muscle, and purified protein systems; population-specific limits apply.
- **Methods**: Tracer incorporation, biopsies, molecular perturbation, protein binding, structural biology, and longitudinal tissue measurements.
- **Major limitations**: Selected foundational literature; no exhaustive search or quantitative synthesis. Much of the sensing mechanism comes from systems other than intact exercising human muscle.
- **Confidence**: Well supported for the existence of the cellular mechanisms and acute human responses; limited for the quantitative contribution of each sensor to long-term human adaptation.

## Neighboring concepts

- **Amino acid** — [Leucine](../concepts/leucine.html): An essential amino acid used in proteins and involved in nutrient sensing.

- **Molecular sensor** — [Sestrin2](../concepts/sestrin2.html): A stress-responsive protein that can bind leucine and regulate the nutrient input to mTORC1.

- **Small GTPases** — [Rag GTPases](../concepts/rag-gtpases.html): Heterodimeric nucleotide-binding proteins that help recruit mTORC1 to lysosomal membranes.

- **Small GTPase** — [Rheb](../concepts/rheb.html): A small GTPase that can directly stimulate the catalytic activity of mTORC1.

- **Kinase complex** — [mTORC1](../concepts/mtorc1.html): A protein kinase complex integrating nutrient and regulatory inputs with cellular growth-related processes.

- **Cellular process** — [Muscle protein synthesis](../concepts/muscle-protein-synthesis.html): Production of new muscle proteins from amino acids through translation.

- **Tissue adaptation** — [Hypertrophy](../concepts/hypertrophy.html): A sustained increase in tissue or cell size; here, growth of skeletal muscle fibers and muscle tissue.
