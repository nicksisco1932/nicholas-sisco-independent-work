## The scientific question

Can a short-lived synthesis or signaling response predict muscle growth?

### What changed

An editorial correction of a common inference, revisiting 2014 and 2016 studies; no new experiment is announced.

<aside class="callout"><h2>The foundation</h2><p>A signal, a production rate, and a lasting change in muscle size are different observations. Each requires its own measurement.</p></aside>

<h2 id="read-the-endpoint">Read the endpoint before the conclusion</h2>

### Level 1 · Foundations

First ask what was measured: a molecular mark, new protein, muscle size, or strength.

### Level 2 · Mechanism

A causal hypothesis can connect these endpoints, but measuring one does not establish changes in all the others.

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced — Read the endpoint before the conclusion</summary><p>Prediction and mediation are separate problems. A necessary process can be a poor predictor if it is measured over the wrong interval or if other processes account for outcome variation. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-mitchell2014">Mitchell CJ 2014</a></span></p></details>

## Advanced mechanism in context

Phosphorylation is a regulatory state; tracer incorporation estimates synthesis flux. Neither directly measures the synthesis-minus-breakdown balance accumulated over a training program. [Mitchell CJ 2014](#ref-mitchell2014); [Damas F 2016](#ref-damas2016)

## Why it matters

A study can demonstrate a real biological response while leaving a performance or growth claim unanswered.

<h2 id="appraisal">Evidence & limits</h2>

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Human intervention</span><span>Human biopsy</span><span>Tracer study</span></div><p class="small">Model: Human · Directness: Direct</p><p>Human studies assess synthesis and growth over different timescales; the relation depends on study context. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-mitchell2014">Mitchell CJ 2014</a>; <a href="#ref-damas2016">Damas F 2016</a></span></p></div>

- **What the studies demonstrated**: Mitchell and colleagues found no correlation between the initial acute synthesis response and subsequent hypertrophy in their cohort. Damas and colleagues found a relationship for integrated synthesis after early damage attenuated.
- **What they did not demonstrate**: These studies do not show that synthesis is irrelevant, that all biomarkers are useless, or that phosphorylation can never inform mechanism.
- **Study type**: Concept correction drawing on longitudinal human resistance-training studies.
- **Population / model**: Young men in the cited training cohorts; findings are not automatically generalizable to women, older adults, or clinical populations.
- **Methods**: Stable-isotope synthesis estimates, muscle sampling, and longitudinal hypertrophy measures, with differing integration windows.
- **Major limitations**: Small, specific cohorts; different time windows; associations do not identify every causal mediator. This is a selected comparison, not a meta-analysis.
- **Confidence**: High confidence that these endpoints are distinct. Limited confidence in predicting individual growth from any one acute measurement.
