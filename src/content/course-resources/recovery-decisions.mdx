## How to use it

- Describe the change: ordinary tiredness, new pain, persistent underperformance or illness symptoms. Note duration and context.
- For a manageable temporary constraint, choose a shorter/lighter session, comfortable activity or rest. Use symptoms and technique, not a calendar alone.
- Persistent unexplained deterioration merits appropriate professional assessment. Chest pain, fainting or severe unusual breathlessness calls for stopping and urgent assessment.

<aside class="callout"><h2>Worked example</h2><p>After a short night, a usual warm-up feels unusually demanding. Option: keep the familiar exercises but use one easy set, or rest. Reassess next time. This does not establish overtraining syndrome or a hormone problem.</p></aside>

## Evidence & reading

1. [ECSS/ACSM overtraining consensus (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23247672/) 
ConsensusPopulation: Athletes with prolonged underperformance. Endpoint: Overreaching / overtraining assessment.Limit: No single diagnostic symptom or biomarker; differential diagnosis required.
2. [AASM / SRS sleep duration consensus (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4442216/) 
ConsensusPopulation: Healthy adults. Endpoint: Sleep duration and health.Limit: Individual sleep needs vary; quality and symptoms also matter.
