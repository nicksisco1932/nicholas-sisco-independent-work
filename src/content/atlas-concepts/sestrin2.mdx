## Level 1 · Foundations

Cells use binding proteins to detect chemicals. Sestrin2 links information about leucine to a larger regulatory network.

## Level 2 · Mechanism

Without bound leucine, Sestrin2 can associate with and restrain GATOR2. Leucine binding relieves this interaction, influencing the GATOR1–Rag axis. [Wolfson RL 2016](#ref-wolfson2016)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced</summary><p>This is regulation through protein interactions, not direct phosphorylation of mTOR by leucine. Sensor isoforms and cellular context matter when extrapolating the route to muscle. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-wolfson2016">Wolfson RL 2016</a></span></p></details>

## Inputs

- Intracellular leucine
- Stress context

## Outputs

- GATOR2 regulation

## Necessary versus sufficient

- **Contributory**: A defined leucine-sensing route in experimental cells; not established as the sole human muscle sensor.

## Human evidence

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Mechanistic inference</span></div><p class="small">Model: Human · Directness: Inferred</p><p>Human feeding responses do not identify Sestrin2 as their uniquely necessary mediator. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-churchward2012">Churchward-Venne TA 2012</a></span></p></div>

## Model-system evidence

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Cell culture</span><span>Biochemical reconstitution</span></div><p class="small">Model: Cellular · Directness: Direct</p><p>Leucine-binding mutations and cellular perturbations support the sensing mechanism. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-wolfson2016">Wolfson RL 2016</a></span></p></div>

## What remains unresolved

Isoform contributions and regulation during repeated human exercise need direct testing.

<aside class="callout"><h2>A common oversimplification</h2><p class="misconception">"Sestrin2 is the muscle-growth switch."</p><p><strong>Correction:</strong> It regulates one nutrient input in a network with additional sensors and constraints.</p></aside>

## Practical relevance

Use it to understand a mechanism, not to infer a supplement outcome.
