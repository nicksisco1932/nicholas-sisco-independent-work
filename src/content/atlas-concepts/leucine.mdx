## Level 1 · Foundations

Leucine is one of the building blocks in dietary and body proteins. Humans must obtain it from food; it also helps cells detect amino-acid availability.

## Level 2 · Mechanism

Leucine binding to Sestrin2 influences GATOR-dependent regulation upstream of Rag GTPases and mTORC1 recruitment. [Wolfson RL 2016](#ref-wolfson2016)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced</summary><p>Binding to a sensor and incorporation into a peptide are different chemical events. Sensor occupancy cannot supply the other amino acids needed to complete a protein sequence. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-wolfson2016">Wolfson RL 2016</a>; <a href="#ref-churchward2012">Churchward-Venne TA 2012</a></span></p></details>

## Inputs

- Protein digestion
- Transport
- Protein turnover

## Outputs

- Amino-acid sensing
- Protein substrate supply

## Necessary versus sufficient

- **Required**: As a substrate for leucine-containing proteins.
- **Contributory**: As a nutrient signal; leucine alone is not sufficient for sustained accretion.

## What remains unresolved

The contribution of individual sensors in adult human muscle cannot be read directly from plasma leucine.

<aside class="callout"><h2>A common oversimplification</h2><p class="misconception">"Leucine turns on muscle growth."</p><p><strong>Correction:</strong> Nutrient sensing is one input; synthesis also requires other substrates, and growth requires sustained net accretion.</p></aside>
