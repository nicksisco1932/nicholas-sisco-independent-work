## Level 1 · Foundations

Some signals work by bringing proteins into the same neighborhood. The Rags help position mTORC1 where activation can occur.

## Level 2 · Mechanism

RagA or RagB pairs with RagC or RagD. Nutrient-responsive nucleotide states favor binding to RAPTOR, a component of mTORC1. [Sancak Y 2008](#ref-sancak2008)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced</summary><p>The recruitment-favoring configuration is RagA/B–GTP with RagC/D–GDP. Ragulator anchors the machinery; nucleotide-dependent interfaces control docking rather than directly activating the kinase. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-docking2019">Rogala KB 2019</a></span></p></details>

## Inputs

- GATOR regulation
- Other amino-acid sensing routes
- Nucleotide exchange and hydrolysis

## Outputs

- mTORC1 localization

## Necessary versus sufficient

- **Permissive**: Recruitment in the canonical nutrient pathway; docking alone is not sufficient for kinase output.

## What remains unresolved

Alternative localization routes and tissue-specific regulation complicate a universal pathway diagram.

<aside class="callout"><h2>A common oversimplification</h2><p class="misconception">"Rags activate mTORC1 by themselves."</p><p><strong>Correction:</strong> Recruitment and catalytic activation are distinct regulatory steps.</p></aside>
