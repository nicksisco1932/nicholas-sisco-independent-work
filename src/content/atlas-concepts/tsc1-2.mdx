## Level 1 · Foundations

A cell needs ways to limit costly construction. The TSC complex helps regulate whether a growth-associated signal is transmitted.

## Level 2 · Mechanism

TSC2 supplies GTPase-activating function toward Rheb; TSC1 helps stabilize the complex. AKT-dependent regulation can relieve this restraint. [Garami A 2003](#ref-garami2003); [Inoki K 2002](#ref-inoki2002)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced</summary><p>TSC is not a binary object: phosphorylation, complex integrity, and localization affect regulation. A measurement at one phosphosite cannot report all of these states. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-inoki2002">Inoki K 2002</a></span></p></details>

## Inputs

- Growth-factor signaling
- Energy and stress context

## Outputs

- Rheb nucleotide state

## Necessary versus sufficient

- **Contributory**: A negative regulatory input into Rheb; loss of restraint does not supply growth substrates.

## Human evidence

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Mechanistic inference</span></div><p class="small">Model: Human · Directness: Inferred</p><p>Cellular TSC perturbations cannot quantify normal exercise adaptation in humans. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-inoki2002">Inoki K 2002</a></span></p></div>

## Model-system evidence

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Cell culture</span></div><p class="small">Model: Cellular · Directness: Direct</p><p>Experiments link AKT phosphorylation of TSC2 to regulation of mTOR signaling. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-inoki2002">Inoki K 2002</a></span></p></div>

## What remains unresolved

The timing and contribution of TSC relocalization under distinct human training conditions remain open.

<aside class="callout"><h2>A common oversimplification</h2><p class="misconception">"Removing an inhibitory signal guarantees hypertrophy."</p><p><strong>Correction:</strong> Removing restraint is different from supplying the conditions needed for sustained growth.</p></aside>

## Practical relevance

Interpret relief of inhibition as one mechanistic step.
