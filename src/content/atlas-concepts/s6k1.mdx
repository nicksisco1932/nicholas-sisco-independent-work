## Level 1 · Foundations

One regulatory protein can change the activity of others. S6K1 is part of this relay and is often measured in muscle biopsies.

## Level 2 · Mechanism

mTORC1-dependent phosphorylation contributes to S6K1 regulation. S6K1 acts on downstream substrates, including ribosomal protein S6, within a wider translation network. [Drummond MJ 2009](#ref-drummond2009)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced</summary><p>A phosphosite signal depends on sampling time, protein abundance, and assay normalization. It is not a direct count of new peptide bonds or active ribosomes. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-drummond2009">Drummond MJ 2009</a>; <a href="#ref-thoreen2012">Thoreen CC 2012</a></span></p></details>

## Inputs

- mTORC1-associated signaling
- Other activating inputs

## Outputs

- Substrate phosphorylation
- Translation-associated regulation

## Necessary versus sufficient

- **Correlated**: Often measured alongside synthesis; a phosphorylation change alone is not sufficient evidence of synthesis or hypertrophy.

## Human evidence

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Human biopsy</span></div><p class="small">Model: Human · Directness: Indirect</p><p>S6K1 phosphorylation is responsive in human exercise experiments; its measurement is not a tissue-growth endpoint. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-drummond2009">Drummond MJ 2009</a></span></p></div>

## Model-system evidence

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Cell culture</span></div><p class="small">Model: Cellular · Directness: Indirect</p><p>Translation studies show mTORC1 output is distributed across effectors, including the 4E-BP arm. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-thoreen2012">Thoreen CC 2012</a></span></p></div>
