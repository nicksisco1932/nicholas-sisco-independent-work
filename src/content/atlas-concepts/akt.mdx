## Level 1 · Foundations

Hormones can change how a cell handles nutrients. AKT is one of the proteins carrying those instructions inside the cell.

## Level 2 · Mechanism

Insulin-associated PI3K signaling recruits AKT to membranes. AKT can phosphorylate TSC2, connecting growth-factor information to mTOR regulation. [Inoki K 2002](#ref-inoki2002)

<details class="advanced"><summary>Level 3 · Advanced</summary><p>AKT isoforms, substrate availability, and location shape the response. An AKT phosphosite is a snapshot of regulatory state, not a complete measurement of substrate flux. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-inoki2002">Inoki K 2002</a></span></p></details>

## Inputs

- Insulin and growth factors
- PI3K-dependent membrane lipids

## Outputs

- TSC2 regulation
- Metabolic signaling

## Necessary versus sufficient

- **Contributory**: A growth-factor route; do not assume all loading-induced mTORC1 regulation passes through AKT.

## Human evidence

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Human biopsy</span></div><p class="small">Model: Human · Directness: Indirect</p><p>Exercise signaling measurements help describe responses but cannot establish a universal linear AKT-to-growth chain. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-drummond2009">Drummond MJ 2009</a></span></p></div>

## Model-system evidence

<div class="evidence"><div class="badges"><span>Cell culture</span></div><p class="small">Model: Cellular · Directness: Direct</p><p>AKT–TSC2 regulation has been tested by molecular perturbation. <span class="citations"><a href="#ref-inoki2002">Inoki K 2002</a></span></p></div>

## What remains unresolved

The relative contribution of growth-factor and mechanical inputs is context dependent.

<aside class="callout"><h2>A common oversimplification</h2><p class="misconception">"Resistance exercise grows muscle only by increasing insulin signaling."</p><p><strong>Correction:</strong> Loading and nutrient inputs converge through several routes.</p></aside>

## Practical relevance

Do not equate a hormone excursion with an adaptation.
