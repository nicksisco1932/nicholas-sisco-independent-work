## Schedule

Start with two nonconsecutive days, alternating A and B; for example Tuesday and Saturday. Repeat this schedule as long as useful. If two days are comfortable and recovery permits, an optional third nonconsecutive session alternates A/B/A one week and B/A/B the next. It is not required.

## Warm up

Use about 5–8 minutes of easy walking or similar movement, followed by a few unloaded or very light repetitions of each exercise. A warm-up helps you rehearse and assess today’s comfort; it does not guarantee injury prevention.

## Starting dose

For the first exposures, use one set per exercise at the low end of its repetition range. When the session is repeatable and comfortable, use two sets. Rest about 2 minutes between demanding sets, longer if needed to regain comfortable breathing and control. This conservative starting design is not a claim of an optimal dose.

## Effort

Finish a set while you believe roughly 3–4 controlled repetitions remain possible. This estimate is imprecise for beginners; do not test it by going to failure. If repetitions slow sharply, technique changes or you are unsure, stop the set and choose an easier version next time.

## Progression

When both sets reach the top of the range with similar control and reserve across two sessions, choose the smallest practical increase in resistance. Return toward the bottom of the range. For bodyweight exercise, change one leverage or range variable instead. If the increase disrupts control, return to the earlier version.

## Regression and time constraints

Reduce load, range, sets or exercise difficulty when the warm-up suggests a less demanding day. For a short session, perform one comfortable set of each selected movement instead of rushing rests. Missing a session does not require doubling the next one.

## Comfort and stopping

Muscular effort differs from sharp, worsening or unfamiliar pain. Stop an exercise when pain or technique deteriorates; do not use the course to rehabilitate an injury. Stop activity and seek urgent assessment for chest pain, fainting or severe unusual breathlessness. Obtain appropriate advice when a health condition, pregnancy, recent procedure or symptoms affect exercise choices.

## Activity beyond lifting

Include walking or another comfortable aerobic activity you enjoy. Adult public-health guidance includes 150 minutes of moderate activity weekly and strengthening on at least two days; build toward an appropriate amount gradually. A starter lifting schedule is not a complete account of all physical activity needs.

## Exercise instructions

### Squat to a chair

<span id="exercise-squat"></span>

Squat

Use a stable chair against a wall; clear the floor. For leg press, ask staff to show seat and safety settings.

- Place feet in a comfortable stance and keep the whole foot supported.
- Bend knees and hips together; lightly touch the chair without dropping.
- Stand with control. Use a range you can repeat.

Make it easier: Use a higher stable seat or light hand support.

Progress when ready: Add repetitions, then a small handheld load, only while control remains similar.

Stop for sharp or increasing joint pain, dizziness or loss of balance.

### Hip hinge

<span id="exercise-hinge"></span>

Hinge

Stand with soft knees and a light load close to your thighs; practice without weight first.

- Move the hips back as the torso inclines; keep the load close.
- Keep the trunk controlled rather than rounding to reach the floor.
- Stop the lowering phase when you can no longer keep that position, then stand.

Make it easier: Practice the hip-back motion without weight and shorten the range.

Progress when ready: Increase range only with control; then add repetitions or a small load.

Stop for back pain, radiating symptoms or an uncontrolled position.

### Incline push-up

<span id="exercise-push"></span>

Horizontal push

Choose a fixed, non-slip surface that supports your weight. On machines, confirm seat and handle settings.

- Keep a straight, comfortable trunk position.
- Lower chest toward the support with elbows in a comfortable path.
- Press away without bouncing or shrugging into pain.

Make it easier: Use a higher support such as a wall.

Progress when ready: Lower the support gradually or use a small machine-load increase.

Stop if the support moves or shoulder/wrist pain increases.

### Supported row

<span id="exercise-row"></span>

Horizontal pull

Use a stable bench or fixed counter for support; never pull against an improvised furniture anchor.

- Keep the trunk still and shoulder comfortable.
- Pull the elbow toward your side without twisting to lift the load.
- Return slowly through a controlled range.

Make it easier: Reduce load or use a chest-supported machine.

Progress when ready: Add repetitions before a small load increase.

Stop for painful pulling, loss of support or uncontrolled trunk rotation.

### Supported split squat

<span id="exercise-split"></span>

Single-leg squat

Hold a fixed rail or stable support. Stagger your feet enough to feel balanced.

- Lower a short comfortable distance by bending both knees.
- Keep the front foot supported and control your balance.
- Push through the front foot to rise; repeat on the other side.

Make it easier: Shorten the range or substitute the chair squat.

Progress when ready: Add range or a light dumbbell only after balance and control are repeatable.

Stop for knee/hip pain or loss of balance.

### Glute bridge

<span id="exercise-bridge"></span>

Hip extension

Lie on a mat with knees bent and feet comfortably near your hips; confirm machine setup if used.

- Gently brace and raise your hips without arching aggressively.
- Finish where the hips can extend comfortably.
- Lower with control; avoid pushing into back pain.

Make it easier: Use a smaller lift with no added load.

Progress when ready: Add repetitions before considering a securely positioned light load.

Stop for back pain, cramping that does not settle or discomfort from added load.

### Comfortable overhead press

<span id="exercise-press"></span>

Vertical push

Choose a stable seat and a comfortable arm path. If overhead movement is uncomfortable, use the push alternative.

- Start with light weights near shoulder level.
- Press through a comfortable range without leaning back to finish.
- Lower with control, breathing throughout.

Make it easier: Use lighter loads or the incline push-up.

Progress when ready: Add repetitions then the smallest available load increase.

Stop for shoulder pain or an inability to keep the trunk controlled.

### Pulldown or row

<span id="exercise-pull"></span>

Vertical pull / alternative pull

Check band integrity and the manufacturer’s anchor instructions. If no secure anchor exists, choose a row.

- Keep the trunk mostly still.
- Pull toward the upper chest in a comfortable path; never behind the neck.
- Return slowly without letting the resistance yank your arms.

Make it easier: Use a lighter band/load or a supported row.

Progress when ready: Increase repetitions or resistance while maintaining the same range.

Stop if the anchor shifts, the band is damaged or shoulder pain appears.

### Supported calf raise

<span id="exercise-calf"></span>

Ankle

Stand on a flat floor initially; keep a fixed support within reach.

- Rise onto the forefoot with control.
- Pause briefly without tipping the ankles outward.
- Lower gently; use both legs initially.

Make it easier: Use more hand support or a seated variation.

Progress when ready: Add repetitions before light external load.

Stop for Achilles/ankle pain or loss of balance.

### Light carry

<span id="exercise-carry"></span>

Trunk / carrying

Choose a clear short path and a load you can hold without leaning.

- Walk slowly with shoulders comfortable and trunk upright.
- Breathe normally; avoid rushing turns.
- Set the weight down with control.

Make it easier: Use a lighter load or a stationary hold beside a stable support.

Progress when ready: Add a little duration before load; keep posture similar.

Stop for dizziness, grip failure or pain.

### Dead-bug heel slide

<span id="exercise-deadbug"></span>

Trunk

Lie on your back with knees bent; use a mat and a comfortable trunk position.

- Gently brace while breathing.
- Slide one heel away only as far as you can keep the trunk still.
- Return and alternate; move slowly.

Make it easier: Shorten the slide or practice breathing/bracing without leg movement.

Progress when ready: Extend the slide gradually; quality comes before range.

Stop for back pain or difficulty breathing comfortably.
