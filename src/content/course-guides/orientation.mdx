## Who this is for

This course is for generally healthy adult beginners and returning trainees. It teaches scientific reasoning and practical planning. It does not provide individual medical treatment, injury rehabilitation, pediatric guidance or a guaranteed body-composition result.

## Your first ten minutes

- Choose the three-, six- or twelve-week route. All content is open, and you can change routes or repeat a week.
- Identify your available time, food access, equipment and one ability-based goal. You do not need a wearable or supplements.
- Read the starter guide before performing exercise assignments. Every practice also has a scenario alternative.
- Begin Week 1. Take notes privately on paper if useful; the website saves only optional lesson completion.

<aside class="callout"><h2>How to use the three lessons</h2><p>Understand introduces the idea. Apply turns it into a decision or activity. Review tests the reasoning. Estimated times include reflection; your pace may differ.</p></aside>

## A small glossary

Stimulus

A demand or input, such as mechanical loading.

Response / adaptation

A response occurs around an exposure; adaptation is change that develops across time.

Set / repetition

A repetition is one movement cycle; a set is a group performed before resting.

Repetitions in reserve

An estimate of further controlled repetitions you could perform; beginners need not test this at failure.

Evidence endpoint

What a study measured—such as synthesis, strength or muscle size.

## Choose your level of participation

Perform comfortable activities, use a fictional case, or combine both. Recording food or body measurements is not required. If tracking becomes distressing, use the scenario route. Progress marks are a convenience, not a judgment or verified credential.

If health conditions, pregnancy, symptoms, recent surgery or injury affect activity choices, use appropriate professional guidance. The starter guide explains setup and stopping criteria. Seek direct instruction when equipment or technique is unfamiliar.
